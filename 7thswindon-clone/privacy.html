Privacy notice

How we look after your information

Last updated 17 November 2026.

This notice explains what personal information 7th Swindon Scout Group collects, how we use it, and the rights you have. It applies to our website and to the everyday running of Scouting in our Group.

Who we are and who is responsible

7th Swindon Scout Group is a charity registered in England and Wales, charity number 306101. We are part of Swindon North Scout District and of The Scout Association.

For the information we hold locally, such as website enquiries and volunteering interest, 7th Swindon Scout Group is the data controller. Membership records for young people and adults are held in a national system provided by The Scout Association, and for those records The Scout Association is also a data controller. You can read how The Scout Association handles your information in its data protection policy, linked at the end of this notice.

The information we collect

We only collect what we need. Depending on how you take part, this may include:

  • •Enquiries. When you use our contact form, we collect your name, your email address, and the message you send us.
  • •Volunteering interest. When you tell us you would like to help, we collect your name, your contact details, and the roles or sections you are interested in.
  • •Joining a section. When a young person joins, we collect the details needed to run Scouting safely. This includes the young person's name and date of birth, a parent or carer's contact details, and any health, dietary or additional needs we must know about to keep your child safe and included. These records are held in Online Scout Manager, described below.
  • •Photographs and videos. With permission, we sometimes take photographs or short videos at meetings and events.
  • •Website information. Our website uses a small number of technical cookies, explained in the cookies section below. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies.

Why we use your information and our lawful basis

We use personal information to run Scouting and to respond to you. The main reasons are:

  • •To reply to your enquiries and to keep in touch about volunteering. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in responding to people who contact us.
  • •To run sections safely, plan activities, and contact parents and carers. Our lawful basis is our legitimate interest in delivering Scouting, and, where the law requires it, meeting our legal obligations.
  • •To look after the health, safety and wellbeing of young people. Where we hold health or additional needs information, we rely on your consent and on our duty to safeguard young people.
  • •To share photographs in our newsletters, on our website or on social media. We rely on consent, which you can withdraw at any time.

Online Scout Manager

We use Online Scout Manager, a membership system used widely across Scouting, to hold member records securely. Online Scout Manager processes this information on behalf of our Group and The Scout Association under strict agreements. Access is limited to the leaders and helpers who need it for their role.

Cookies and this website

Our website is deliberately light. It sets only the cookies needed for it to work and to protect our forms from spam:

  • •A security check from Cloudflare Turnstile helps us tell real visitors from automated spam when you use our forms. This is necessary for the forms to work.
  • •We do not use website analytics, advertising cookies, or any other tracking.

Because these cookies are necessary for the website and its forms to function, we do not show a cookie consent banner. You can still block cookies in your browser settings, although our forms may then stop working.

Who we share your information with

We do not sell your information, and we do not share it for marketing. We share information only where we need to:

  • •With The Scout Association and Online Scout Manager, as described above, to maintain membership records.
  • •With district or county Scouting, where this is needed for events, awards or permits.
  • •Where we are required to by law, or where it is necessary to protect a young person or an adult from harm.

How long we keep your information

We keep information only for as long as we need it. Enquiry and volunteering messages are kept while we deal with them and for a short period afterwards. Membership records are kept for as long as the person is involved in Scouting, and for a limited period afterwards in line with The Scout Association's retention guidance, for example to support awards, safeguarding and insurance. After that we securely delete or anonymise it.

Keeping your information safe

We take sensible steps to protect your information. Access to member records is restricted to the leaders and helpers who need it, systems are password protected, and we follow The Scout Association's digital and data protection standards.

Children and young people

Much of the information we hold relates to children. We collect it from a parent or carer, use it only to run Scouting safely, and give it the extra care it deserves. If you have any question about your child's information, please contact us.

Your rights

Under data protection law you have the right to:

  • •ask for a copy of the information we hold about you,
  • •ask us to correct anything that is wrong,
  • •ask us to delete information where there is no good reason for us to keep it,
  • •object to, or ask us to limit, how we use your information,
  • •withdraw any consent you have given, for example for photographs.

To use any of these rights, please contact us using the details below. We will normally respond within one month.

How to contact us

If you have a question about this notice, or you would like to make a request about your information, please get in touch through our contact form. For membership records held by The Scout Association, you can also contact them directly using the policy linked below.

Contact us

How to complain

We will always try to put things right if you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, so please tell us first. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the United Kingdom regulator for data protection. You can reach the Information Commissioner's Office through its website at ico.org.uk, or by calling its helpline on 0303 123 1113.
